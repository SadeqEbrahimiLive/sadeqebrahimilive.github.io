Research Directions

My research interests are centered around signal processing methods for next-generation wireless communication and sensing systems. I am interested in developing efficient algorithms that combine mathematical modeling, statistical inference, and learning-based approaches to address challenges in dynamic and high-dimensional wireless environments.

The main themes of my research include spatial signal processing, sparse estimation, advanced waveform design, and adaptive learning methods. These areas provide fundamental tools for improving spectral efficiency, reliability, sensing capability, and computational efficiency in future wireless systems.

Array and Spatial Signal Processing

Research Motivation

Modern wireless systems increasingly rely on large antenna arrays to exploit spatial degrees of freedom. Accurate estimation of spatial information is essential for applications such as beamforming, localization, multi-user communication, and integrated sensing and communication.

Research Problem

A practical challenge in array processing is that measurements are affected by noise, interference, clutter, and model mismatch. In particular, direction-of-arrival estimation methods based on predefined angular grids may suffer from performance degradation when actual source locations do not align with the assumed grid.

Research Contribution

My M.S. research investigated off-grid direction-of-arrival estimation under measurement noise and heavy clutter conditions. The research focused on improving estimation accuracy and robustness by addressing limitations of conventional sparse recovery approaches.

Sparse and Structured Signal Processing

Research Motivation

Many modern communication and sensing systems generate high-dimensional data while only a limited number of parameters contain significant information. Sparse signal processing provides a mathematical framework for recovering meaningful information using fewer measurements and reduced computational resources.

Research Challenges

Important challenges include robustness against noise, model uncertainty, computational complexity, and the design of algorithms that exploit the structure of real-world signals.

Research Direction

My interests include sparse estimation methods for wireless systems, structured recovery problems, and efficient algorithms for parameter estimation in communication and sensing applications.

Advanced Waveforms and Faster-than-Nyquist Signaling

Research Motivation

The rapid growth of wireless traffic creates continuous demand for higher spectral efficiency. Faster-than-Nyquist signaling explores the possibility of transmitting symbols closer than the conventional Nyquist interval while maintaining reliable communication through appropriate signal processing.

Technical Challenge

Increasing symbol density introduces controlled intersymbol interference, creating a trade-off between spectral efficiency and receiver complexity. Effective waveform design and detection strategies are required to exploit the available bandwidth efficiently.

Research Activity

My recent work investigates pulse-shaping design for Faster-than-Nyquist signaling, focusing on improving spectral efficiency while managing the interference introduced by intentional symbol overlap.

Learning-Based Wireless Signal Processing

Research Motivation

Future wireless systems operate in highly dynamic environments where channel conditions, user mobility, and interference patterns continuously change. Learning-based methods provide opportunities for adaptive prediction and optimization beyond traditional model-based approaches.

Research Direction

I am interested in combining signal-processing principles with learning methods to develop efficient algorithms for dynamic wireless systems. Particular interests include adaptive learning, nonlinear modeling, predictive signal processing, and computationally efficient approaches suitable for physical-layer applications.

Applications

Potential applications include channel prediction, beam management, adaptive receivers, and intelligent communication and sensing systems.

Integrated Communication and Sensing

Research Motivation

Integrated sensing and communication (ISAC) aims to utilize shared hardware and signal resources to provide both data transmission and environmental awareness. It is considered one of the important directions for future wireless systems.

Research Challenges

ISAC systems require efficient signal processing methods capable of balancing communication performance and sensing accuracy while operating under limited resources and dynamic environments.

Research Interest

My interest is in signal-processing approaches for ISAC systems, including spatial processing, waveform design, and learning-based methods for improving adaptability in communication and sensing scenarios.
