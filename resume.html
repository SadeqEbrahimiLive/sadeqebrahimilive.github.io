Research Profile

Electrical engineer with an M.S. degree in Communication Systems and research experience in statistical, sparse, array, and learning-based signal processing for wireless communications. My research focuses on developing efficient algorithms for communication and sensing systems through mathematical modeling, signal processing, and computational methods.

My research background includes off-grid direction-of-arrival estimation, sparse signal processing, and advanced waveform design. Current interests include adaptive learning methods, intelligent wireless systems, and computationally efficient physical-layer algorithms.

Research Interests

Array and Spatial Signal Processing

Direction estimation, beamforming, localization, and multi-antenna communication and sensing systems.

Sparse and Structured Signal Processing

Sparse recovery, parameter estimation, compressed sensing, and efficient inference methods for high-dimensional systems.

Learning-Based Wireless Signal Processing

Adaptive learning, nonlinear modeling, prediction, and intelligent methods for dynamic communication environments.

Advanced Waveforms and Sensing

Waveform design, Faster-than-Nyquist signaling, and integrated communication and sensing systems.

Independent Research — Wireless Communications and Signal Processing

Current

Investigating adaptive and learning-based signal processing approaches for wireless communication and sensing applications. The research explores efficient algorithms suitable for dynamic environments and practical physical-layer constraints.

  • Studying adaptive learning approaches for predictive wireless signal processing.
  • Exploring computationally efficient learning methods for nonlinear and time-varying systems.
  • Investigating the integration of signal-processing models with learning techniques for future wireless systems.

Graduate Researcher — Communication Systems Lab

Sadjad University of Technology · 2017–2019

Conducted research on sparse array signal processing and direction-of-arrival estimation under practical conditions including measurement noise and heavy clutter.

  • Developed off-grid DOA estimation approaches addressing grid mismatch limitations in sparse recovery methods.
  • Designed MATLAB simulation frameworks for evaluating estimation accuracy, robustness, and computational complexity.
  • Compared proposed approaches with conventional sparse estimation methods.
