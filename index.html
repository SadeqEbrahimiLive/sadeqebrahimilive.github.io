Signal Processing for Wireless Communications and Sensing

I am a wireless communications and signal processing researcher interested in developing efficient algorithms for next-generation communication and sensing systems. My research lies at the intersection of array signal processing, sparse inference, and learning-based methods, with a focus on mathematically grounded and computationally efficient solutions for dynamic wireless environments.

My research contributions include sparse off-grid direction-of-arrival (DOA) estimation, with results published in Signal, Image and Video Processing. I have also investigated advanced waveform design through Faster-than-Nyquist (FTN) pulse shaping, with a manuscript submitted to IEEE Wireless Communications Letters. These works reflect my interest in improving the efficiency, robustness, and performance of physical-layer communication and sensing systems.

My research interests extend to adaptive beamforming, integrated sensing and communication (ISAC), reconfigurable intelligent surfaces (RIS/IRS), non-orthogonal multiple access (NOMA), and learning-based wireless signal processing. I am also interested in applying deep learning approaches, including recurrent architectures such as GRU and LSTM-based models, when they provide effective solutions for wireless communication problems. I remain open to related research directions in wireless communications and signal processing.

Research Directions

Array and Spatial Signal Processing

Developing methods for extracting spatial information from antenna-array measurements, including direction estimation, beamforming, localization, and multi-antenna communication systems.

Sparse and Structured Signal Processing

Exploiting sparsity and structural properties of signals to improve estimation accuracy, reduce measurement requirements, and address high-dimensional wireless signal-processing problems.

Learning-Based Wireless Signal Processing

Investigating adaptive and data-driven methods for nonlinear and dynamic wireless environments, including efficient learning approaches for prediction and optimization.

Advanced Waveforms and Sensing

Studying waveform design and signal-processing techniques that improve spectral efficiency and enable integrated communication and sensing capabilities.

Selected Research

Off-Grid DOA Estimation

My M.S. research investigated sparse off-grid DOA estimation using an Expectation–Maximization (EM)-based framework to address grid mismatch effects and improve robustness against measurement noise and heavy clutter in array signal-processing systems.

Pulse-Shaping Design for Faster-than-Nyquist Signaling

This work investigates advanced pulse-shaping design for Faster-than-Nyquist (FTN) signaling to improve spectral efficiency while maintaining reliable communication performance. A parameterized Cardinal Exponential-Spline (CES) pulse family is explored as an alternative to conventional pulse shapes, providing flexible control over spectral characteristics. A multi-objective optimization framework is employed to balance spectral efficiency, out-of-band energy, and folded-spectrum behavior while preserving communication performance under practical receiver conditions.

Adaptive Learning for Wireless Signal Processing

Current research investigates adaptive learning approaches for predictive wireless signal processing in dynamic communication and sensing environments. The work explores how kernel-based and data-driven methods can learn complex system relationships from available observations to support efficient decision-making in ISAC scenarios, including predictive beamforming and resource optimization.

Research Approach

My research approach focuses on developing signal-processing solutions that connect theoretical analysis with practical wireless system requirements. I begin by studying the limitations of existing methods and identifying the assumptions that restrict their performance in realistic scenarios, such as noise, uncertainty, interference, and computational constraints.

Based on these observations, I develop mathematical models and efficient algorithms, combining analytical approaches with numerical evaluation to investigate their effectiveness. I am also interested in exploring how learning-based techniques can complement traditional signal-processing methods to improve adaptability and performance in dynamic communication and sensing environments.

From Algorithms to Implementation

Beyond theoretical algorithm development, I am interested in understanding how signal-processing methods can evolve from mathematical concepts and simulations into practical communication systems. This curiosity motivates me to explore implementation-oriented platforms where algorithms can be evaluated under realistic constraints such as computational complexity, latency, and hardware limitations.

Software-defined radio and FPGA-based platforms provide valuable environments for academic research by enabling experimental validation of communication algorithms and creating a connection between theoretical models and real-world system behavior. I am particularly interested in exploring how platforms such as GNU Radio, FPGA architectures, and high-level synthesis workflows can support the development and evaluation of efficient wireless signal-processing solutions.
